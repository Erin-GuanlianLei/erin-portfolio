Communication Strategy & Overview

As part of the broader communication strategy, the social media component positions tourists as active participants in Great Southern Reef experiences, encouraging engagement through food, diving, and related activities.

This communication strategy uses two complementary social media platforms, Instagram and TikTok, to engage different audience groups with the Great Southern Reef (GSR).

Instagram Visual Strategy: Instagram is a highly visual platform focused on lifestyle, travel, and food content. Its audience is broader, ranging from young adults to middle-aged users, making it effective for influencing lifestyle interests and tourism decisions. The carousel presents long-spined sea urchins as a sustainable seafood experience, using strong imagery, brief captions, and location tagging. This links environmental awareness with consumption and tourism behaviour.

TikTok Narrative Strategy: TikTok is a short-form, algorithm-driven platform that prioritises storytelling, entertainment, and rapid sharing. The TikTok series follows Wendy, a young weedy seadragon, using episodic storytelling to communicate ecological change. Through dialogue, underwater visuals, and narrative structure, it introduces issues such as kelp forest decline and the impact of long-spined sea urchins.

Instagram Carousel ("Eat an Urchin")

An Instagram carousel supporting the existing “Eat an Urchin” campaign, presenting long-spined sea urchin as a unique Tasmanian food experience while connecting tourism with conservation.

TikTok Narrative Series: Wendy of the Great Southern Reef

The submitted content is a detailed script for the first episode, including character descriptions, dialogue, narration, and production directions.

Character List & Introduction

Wendy: A young weedy seadragon. She lives in a coastal kelp forest, highly observant and innately curious. She serves as the emotional heart of the series.

Emma: A yellowtail scad and Wendy’s closest friend. She is the first to alert Wendy to subtle shifts in their marine environment.

William: An adult weedy seadragon and Wendy’s older brother. He carries and broods fertilised eggs beneath his tail, cautious and protective.

Ben: A small goby, bottom-dwelling and reef-bound. Direct, perceptive and plain-spoken, delivering critical hidden information.

Officer Andrew & Sea Urchins: The unseen guardians and the growing ecological threat driving the central conflict.

Episode 1: The Home That Is Disappearing (Part I)

FADE IN: BLACK SCREEN SOUND DESIGN: Low underwater ambient hum. Gentle bubble pops. Slow water current sweeping over rock and kelp fronds. A girl’s voice echoes, distant and muffled. EMMA (V.O., muffled) Wendy… Wendy… Wendy… EMMA (V.O., clear) Wendy! WENDY POV SHOT The screen brightens slowly. Pale aquamarine light filters through silver-blue water. We see entirely through Wendy’s eyes. Kelp fronds drift across the lens. WENDY What? Emma hangs close, her posture tight with quiet anxiety. WENDY Emma? Why are you here? CUT TO THIRD-PERSON VIEW Wendy comes into full view: a young weedy seadragon, half-camouflaged within swaying kelp. EMMA Come with me. Wendy hesitates, then glides after her. In the mid-background, a vast school of yellowtail scad surges as one. The camera rises higher, revealing the reef below — mostly vibrant, but with a stretch in the far distance stripped bare into a barren wasteland. WENDY Why’s everyone leaving the reef? EMMA Because our home’s fading. The kelp isn’t half as thick as it used to be. Sea urchins are spreading further every day, and food’s getting scarcer. We have to move to survive.

Episode 1: The Home That Is Disappearing (Part II)

INT. DENSE KELP PATCH - UNDERWATER William rests anchored among the fronds, calm and steady, holding his cluster of eggs safely tucked beneath his tail. Wendy glides into view. WILLIAM Wendy. You’re awake far earlier than usual. WENDY Emma just came to say goodbye. She told me sea urchins are spreading fast, and this whole kelp forest might be in real danger. WILLIAM Oh, that’s just how schooling fish are. We’re not going anywhere. The reef is still here. The kelp is still thick. BEN (O.S.) That’s exactly the problem. Ben the goby emerges from the sand and rock. BEN I’m Ben. Just a goby that sticks close to the sand. Do you know how many wrasse have gone missing lately? Even Officer Andrew is gone. Without the wrasse hunting juveniles, sea urchins will only spread further. WENDY Emma wasn’t exaggerating at all. BEN She wasn’t. She just chose to leave before it gets worse. FADE TO BLACK.
